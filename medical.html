How it works for New Zealanders

From New Zealand to India and home again

Waiting months for elective surgery, or facing high private costs? Our New Zealand-based lead looks after you at home, and our Delhi team looks after you in India. Both work from the same patient file.

In NZ
Consultation, reports, and consent
Our New Zealand lead meets you, gathers your scans, specialist letters, and GP notes, and records your written consent to share them with hospitals in India.
In India
Specialist review and written estimate
Our Delhi team shares your reports with accredited hospitals, arranges a tele-consultation with the specialist, and gets you a written treatment plan and itemised estimate.
In NZ
Hospital letter, visa, and travel
Using the hospital's invitation letter, you apply for the e-Medical Visa through the official portal. We help with flights, travel insurance questions, and a pre-departure briefing.
In India
Admission, consent, and treatment
Airport pickup, hospital registration, informed consent with your treating doctor, accommodation nearby, and daily updates to your family in New Zealand.
In NZ
Records, medicines, and follow-up
You return with your discharge summary, imaging, and prescriptions in English. Our New Zealand lead helps you hand them to your GP and checks in as you recover.

Indicative hospital costs at accredited Indian hospitals, compared with private treatment in New Zealand, in New Zealand dollars. For most procedures the saving comfortably covers return flights, accommodation, and a companion.

For example, a hip replacement is around NZD 10,000 to 14,000 in India, compared with NZD 30,000 to 40,000 privately in New Zealand. Flights and accommodation are extra. Patient journey

How does medical treatment in India work for New Zealanders?

Six steps, each following the laws that apply in New Zealand and India. One point of contact, backed by our Delhi team in India and our New Zealand lead at home.

Short answer
You share your reports with consent, an accredited hospital reviews your case and sends a written estimate, you apply for an e-Medical Visa using the hospital's letter, you are treated with informed consent from your treating doctor, and you return to New Zealand with your records for your GP. Our New Zealand lead and Delhi team support each step.
Last updated: 2 October 2026
01
Share your reports, with your consent
Send your diagnosis, scans, and specialist letters. Before we share anything with a hospital, you give written consent, as required under New Zealand's Privacy Act 2020 and Health Information Privacy Code and India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act. We are care coordinators, not doctors: we do not diagnose or recommend a treatment.
02
Specialist review and written estimate
A specialist at an NABH or JCI-accredited hospital reviews your reports and can speak with you by tele-consultation. You receive a written treatment plan and itemised estimate from the hospital. Any fee we charge, and any referral commission we receive from the hospital, is disclosed to you in writing up front.
03
Hospital letter, visa, and legal approvals
The treating hospital issues an invitation letter, which India's e-Medical Visa requires. You apply only through the official Indian Visa Online portal; companions apply for an e-Medical Attendant Visa. Some treatments need extra approvals before you travel: organ transplants require hospital Authorisation Committee approval under the Transplantation of Human Organs and Tissues Act, 1994, with donor relationship documents verified through the New Zealand High Commission.
04
Arrival, admission, and informed consent
Our Delhi team meets you and supports your hospital registration. Your treating doctor explains the procedure, risks, and alternatives, and you sign the hospital's informed consent forms directly with them. Clinical decisions always rest with your treating doctor. If your visa requires registration with the Foreigners Regional Registration Office (FRRO), we help you complete it.
05
Treatment and in-hospital support
Our Delhi team supports you at appointments, helps with communication and paperwork, and keeps your family in New Zealand updated, with your permission. You keep the right to ask questions, request a second opinion, or decline treatment at any point.
06
Discharge, records, and fit to fly
Before you leave India you receive a discharge summary, test results, imaging, and prescriptions in English, and your doctor confirms you are fit to fly. Bring prescription medicines home with a doctor's letter: New Zealand generally allows up to three months' supply for personal use. Our New Zealand lead helps you hand your records to your GP and checks in as you recover.
Laws and standards we follow
  • Accredited hospitals only: we refer to NABH or JCI-accredited hospitals and confirm current status before each referral.
  • No medical advice from us: diagnosis, treatment plans, and consent are between you and your treating doctor.
  • Your health information is shared only with your written consent, under New Zealand's Privacy Act 2020 and Health Information Privacy Code and India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023.
  • Organ transplants follow the Transplantation of Human Organs and Tissues Act, 1994, including Authorisation Committee approval. We never arrange paid or unrelated donors.
  • IVF is coordinated only at clinics registered under India's Assisted Reproductive Technology (Regulation) Act, 2021. Commercial surrogacy is prohibited under the Surrogacy (Regulation) Act, 2021, and surrogacy is not available to foreign nationals, so we do not arrange it.
  • Visas are applied for only through official Government of India channels, for the visa type that matches the purpose of travel.
  • Fees and any referral commissions are disclosed in writing before you commit, and we never guarantee clinical outcomes.

Frequently asked

How much does medical treatment in India cost?

For patients from New Zealand, treatment in India typically costs 55 to 85 percent less than private treatment at home. As an indication, a hip replacement costs around NZD 10,000 to 14,000 in India compared with NZD 30,000 to 40,000 privately in New Zealand, and a dental implant around NZD 850 to 1,700 compared with NZD 5,000 to 7,000. Return flights and accommodation are extra, but the overall saving is usually substantial. We send a written estimate in NZD from the recommended hospital within 48 hours of receiving your reports.

How do I apply for an e-Medical Visa for India?

The e-Medical Visa is available to citizens of 167 countries and is applied for online at the Indian Visa Online portal. You need a hospital appointment confirmation letter, passport-size photo, and passport details. Processing typically takes 1 to 4 business days. Companions apply for an e-Medical Attendant Visa; the number allowed per patient is set by the Government of India, and we confirm it when you apply. We manage the full visa documentation process for every patient we coordinate.

Which hospitals in India are best for international patients?

The best hospitals for international patients are those with NABH or JCI accreditation. Leading options include Manipal Hospitals (multi-city, cardiac, orthopaedic, oncology), Max Healthcare (Delhi NCR, cardiac, oncology), Apollo Hospitals (pan-India, widest international network), and Narayana Health (cardiac, Bengaluru). We recommend based on your specific treatment need and preferred city, not by any preferred arrangement.

Do you charge the patient for coordination services?

Our coordination fee model is discussed transparently at the start of each engagement. In most cases we earn a referral commission from the hospital and do not charge the patient directly for coordination. For complex cases requiring significant on-ground support, a patient care coordination fee may apply. This is always disclosed upfront before any commitment is made.

Why are New Zealanders travelling to India for surgery?

Mainly to avoid long waits and high costs. Public waiting lists for elective procedures such as hip and knee replacement can stretch for many months, and private treatment can cost tens of thousands of dollars. Accredited Indian hospitals can usually schedule surgery within weeks of reviewing your reports, at a fraction of the private price, with English-speaking specialists.

Do New Zealand citizens need a visa for medical treatment in India?

Yes. New Zealand passport holders are eligible for India's e-Medical Visa, applied for online with your passport details, a photo, and a letter from the treating hospital. A companion travelling with you applies for an e-Medical Attendant Visa. Our New Zealand-based lead helps you prepare the application before you travel.

Will my New Zealand GP or specialist be involved?

We encourage it. We ask for your existing reports and specialist letters before you travel, and when you return you bring a full discharge summary, imaging, and an aftercare plan to share with your GP. Our New Zealand lead can help you arrange that handover and any follow-up physiotherapy at home.

Is it safe to have surgery in India?

At accredited hospitals, yes. We only refer patients to NABH or JCI-accredited hospitals, which follow audited standards for infection control, patient safety, and clinical governance. Many of India's leading hospitals report outcomes for cardiac and orthopaedic surgery comparable to international benchmarks. We share your reports with specialists before you travel, so you know the treatment plan and the surgeon before you book a flight.

How long will I need to stay in India?

It depends on the treatment. Typical stays are 2 to 3 weeks for hip or knee replacement, 3 to 4 weeks for cardiac bypass, 5 to 10 days for dental implants (with a second visit for some cases), and 2 to 3 weeks for an IVF cycle. Your treating doctor confirms the recommended stay, including time before you are fit to fly, as part of your treatment plan.

Can a family member travel with me?

Yes. Family members travelling with you can apply for an e-Medical Attendant Visa linked to your e-Medical Visa. We arrange accommodation for your companion near the hospital, and our Delhi team keeps them updated at every step.

What happens with follow-up care when I get home?

Before you leave India, you receive your discharge summary, imaging, and a written aftercare plan to share with your doctor at home. Your treating doctor stays available for follow-up tele-consultations. For patients in New Zealand, our New Zealand-based lead checks in after you return and helps coordinate with your GP.

Can you coordinate post-treatment recovery in India before I return home?

Yes. We plan Ayurvedic recovery retreats, physiotherapy programmes, and wellness stays for patients who wish to spend time in India recovering before flying home. Kerala is a popular recovery destination for post-cardiac and post-orthopaedic patients. We also arrange wellness coordination for patients from New Zealand and Australia through our New Zealand retreat partner network.
